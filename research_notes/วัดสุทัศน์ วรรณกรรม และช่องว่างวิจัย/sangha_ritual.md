### Inferences
- The partial lists agree with each other: แดง is the 3rd abbot, แพ the 4th, and แดง was แพ's preceptor. That supports the order from the 3rd to the 8th abbot. Abbots 1 and 2 (Rama III to Rama IV era) are still unknown.
- Possible date gap: แพ died in 1944, but โสม's tenure is given from 1946. The gap may be an acting abbot (ผู้รักษาการ) or an error in the source. It needs checking against the 1973 temple history or the Royal Gazette (ราชกิจจานุเบกษา).
- The 44-year abbacy, with death in 1944, implies he became abbot around 1900 (BE 2443). This is my arithmetic only, not from a source.

### Gaps

- Whether พระธรรมรัตนดิลก (เชิด) is still abbot in 2026, and his current title, could not be confirmed. The latest mention found is from 2018.
- I found no thesis or journal article that analyses the writings or sermons of สมเด็จพระสังฆราช แพ. A dedicated search returned only theses on other Sangharajas (for example, กรมพระปรมานุชิตชิโนรส at Chulalongkorn) ([cuir.car.chula.ac.th/handle/123456789/18790](https://cuir.car.chula.ac.th/handle/123456789/18790), ACAD [T]). - Popular sources describe a Wat Suthat lineage: สมเด็จแพ, then พระมงคลราชมุนี (สนธิ์), with recorded casting manuals and rites. That is a documentable textual and ritual tradition, and it is a feasible thesis subject. Possible angles: the casting manuals (ตำราการสร้างพระกริ่ง) as ritual texts; how Mahayana ไภษัชยคุรุ iconography was absorbed into Thai Theravada; healing-water (น้ำพระพุทธมนต์) practice.
- Note the doctrinal framing. ไภษัชยคุรุ is Mahayana, yet Wat Suthat is a Theravada royal temple of the มหานิกาย. A study would have to handle how a Mahayana iconographic source sits within Theravada practice.

- I found nothing on whether the casting manuals survive or have been published.
- The พระชัยวัฒน์ of Wat Suthat was not reached in any search result.

## Q4. Rituals and festivals at the temple, and its relationship with the Brahmin Devasathan and the Giant Swing (ตรียัมปวาย)

### Takeaway
The Giant Swing stands between Wat Suthat and the Devasathan (โบสถ์พราหมณ์), and the swing rite of ตรียัมปวาย–ตรีปวาย was discontinued in the 1930s. There is international academic work on the royal court Brahmins (McGovern). I found no academic study of the Buddhist–Brahmin relationship at Wat Suthat itself. Material on the temple's own festival calendar was not found.

### Cited Findings
- Nathan McGovern, "Balancing the Foreign and the Familiar in the Articulation of Kingship: The Royal Court Brahmans of Thailand". It argues that the court Brahmins are a localized institution, not simply an Indian import. It centres on the yearly Trīyampawāi–Trīppawāi rite, and notes that the court Brahmins are Buddhist and that some ordain briefly as monks before becoming Brahmins. [S] — [Cambridge Core PDF](https://www.cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/5079FDD2067B360E4CC0A67DE6B1C42F/S002246341700008Xa.pdf/balancing-the-foreign-and-the-familiar-in-the-articulation-of-kingship-the-royal-court-brahmans-of-thailand.pdf) (ACAD). **Journal uncertain:** the search summary says *Journal of the Royal Asiatic Society*, but the identifier S0022463417… carries the ISSN prefix of the *Journal of Southeast Asian Studies* (0022-4634). Confirm before citing.
- Per the same search summary, McGovern cites a 1996 Chulalongkorn MA thesis by Kanjana Suwanwong on the court Brahmins. [LEAD]
- The Giant Swing was built in 2327 (1784) under Rama I. Popular sources usually give 8 April 2327, and some say 1786. Popular sources also say the swing was built for ตรียัมปวาย at the request of พระครูสิทธิชัยกระต่าย. [S] — [gotoknow.org 422618](https://www.gotoknow.org/posts/422618) (POP blog); [amarintv](https://www.amarintv.com/horoscope/article/511118) (NEWS); [en.wikipedia Giant Swing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giant_Swing) (lead only). Dates conflict, so check against Fine Arts Department sources.
- The swinging was discontinued in the reign of Rama VII (1934; some sources say 1935) after accidents. The court Brahmins kept performing the prayers in private at the Devasathan. [S] — [en.wikipedia Giant Swing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giant_Swing) (lead only); [tnews](https://www.tnews.co.th/variety/324766) (NEWS)
- The Fine Arts Department registered the Giant Swing in the Royal Gazette in 2492 (1949), vol. 66, part 64. [S], via Thai Wikipedia summary (lead only). [th.wikipedia เสาชิงช้า](https://th.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E0%B9%80%E0%B8%AA%E0%B8%B2%E0%B8%8A%E0%B8%B4%E0%B8%87%E0%B8%8A%E0%B9%89%E0%B8%B2)
- Silpakorn University Library's Rattanakosin local-knowledge resource has a page on "ชุมชนโบสถ์พราหมณ์". [T] — [resource.lib.su.ac.th](http://www.resource.lib.su.ac.th/rattanakosin/index.php/2014-10-27-08-52-32/2015-10-21-08-18-25) (INST, university library)
- วารสารดำรงวิชาการ has an article on the history of the วิหารหลวง and the พระศรีศากยมุนี of Wat Suthat (ACAD). [S/T] — [so01 damrong 256285](https://so01.tci-thaijo.org/index.php/damrong/article/download/256285/172410/992100). The summary did not say whether Wat Suthat is its main focus.

### Inferences
- Wat Suthat sits next to the Devasathan and the Giant Swing. McGovern shows that the court Brahmins are themselves Buddhist. Together these suggest a researchable question: how monks and Brahmins on adjacent sites have shared or divided ritual roles, for example at ตรียัมปวาย and in ceremonies at the temple. Nothing found addresses this directly.

### Gaps
- I found no academic work on Buddhist–Brahmin interaction specific to Wat Suthat.
- Wat Suthat's own festival calendar was not found: วันวิสาขบูชา events, ทอดกฐิน หลวง, rites at the พระศรีศากยมุนี, and พิธีเททองหล่อพระกริ่ง.
- The full text of McGovern was not read. I have its argument only from the search summary.

### Inferences: candidate thesis topics (all provisional, because no database could be opened)
1. **The Wat Suthat พระกริ่ง tradition as Buddhist ritual and doctrine.** The สมเด็จแพ casting manuals and rites, ไภษัชยคุรุ iconography in a Theravada setting, and healing-water practice. The academic literature found covers only the พระกริ่งปวเรศ, while amulet-market media are abundant. Feasible if the temple museum or its archive gives access to the texts.
2. **The writings and sermons of สมเด็จพระสังฆราช (แพ ติสฺสเทว).** No study was found. Feasible if his works can be found, for example cremation volumes (หนังสืออนุสรณ์งานศพ) and the Vajirayana or National Library collections.
3. **Monks and Brahmins on adjacent sites (Wat Suthat, the Devasathan and the Giant Swing).** A localized study building on McGovern. Needs fieldwork and archives; possibly too broad for a short thesis unless narrowed to one ritual.
4. **The Wat Suthat สำนักเรียน.** A data-driven history built from the บาลีสนามหลวง announcements. Feasible if the yearly announcements are accessible.
5. **Present-day roles**: the museum as Dhamma education, and services for Chinese-speaking visitors. Little academic work was found. These lean towards tourism studies more than Buddhist Studies.
